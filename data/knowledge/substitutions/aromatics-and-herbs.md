# Aromatics, Herbs, and Cooking Wine Substitutions

### Fresh Herbs vs. Dried Herbs Conversion
- **General rule of thumb:** 1 tablespoon of chopped fresh herbs = 1 teaspoon of dried herbs (3:1 ratio). Dried herbs are concentrated and have less water volume.
- **Timing:** Add dried herbs early in cooking to release their fat-soluble oils; add fresh leafy herbs (basil, parsley, cilantro) near the end to preserve fragrance and color.

### Substituting Dry White Wine in Cooking
- **Best match:** Low-sodium chicken broth or vegetable broth plus a splash (1/2 teaspoon per 1/4 cup) of fresh lemon juice or white wine vinegar to provide acidity.
- **Alcoholic alternative:** Dry vermouth (keeps for months in the fridge; 1:1 substitute).
- **Culinary caveat:** Never use commercial "cooking wine" with added sodium and preservatives.

### Substituting Dry Red Wine in Cooking
- **Best match:** Beef broth or mushroom stock with 1 teaspoon of red wine vinegar or balsamic vinegar per 1/2 cup of broth.
- **Alternative:** Unsweetened 100% tart cherry juice or pomegranate juice diluted 50/50 with broth.

### Substituting Shallots
- **Best match:** Yellow onion or red onion finely minced + a small pinch of minced garlic (shallots taste like a mild, sweet onion with subtle garlic notes).
- **Alternative:** The white parts of scallions (green onions) or leeks finely chopped.

### Fresh Garlic vs. Garlic Powder
- **Conversion:** 1 fresh medium garlic clove = 1/8 teaspoon garlic powder or 1/4 teaspoon granulated garlic.
- **Culinary caveat:** Fresh garlic provides sharp pungency; garlic powder distributes mellow, toasted sweetness. For sautéing foundations, fresh is strongly preferred.
