# Sauces and Emulsions Techniques

### What is an Emulsion?
- **Definition:** A stable suspension of two liquids that normally do not mix, such as oil (fat) and water (vinegar, broth, lemon juice, or pasta water).
- **Key emulsifiers:** Egg yolks (contain lecithin), mustard, garlic, and starchy pasta cooking water.

### Making Carbonara Without Scrambling Eggs
- **The danger:** Whole eggs coagulate at 65°C–70°C (145°F–160°F). If raw eggs hit a smoking hot skillet directly on the stove, they scramble instantly into an omelet.
- **The technique (off-heat residual cooking):**
  1. Remove the skillet completely from the heat burner.
  2. Toss the hot drained pasta with the rendered pork fat for 30 seconds to let the temperature drop slightly below boiling.
  3. Pour in the beaten egg and grated cheese mixture, tossing and stirring vigorously.
  4. Splash in 2–3 tablespoons of hot starchy pasta water. The residual heat creates a creamy, glossy glaze without cooking curds.

### How to Fix a Broken or Split Sauce
- **Symptom:** Oil separates and pools on top, or sauce looks grainy and curdled.
- **Fix for butter/cream sauce:** Remove from heat and vigorously whisk in 1 to 2 tablespoons of boiling water or heavy cream one teaspoon at a time until re-emulsified.
- **Fix for vinaigrette or hollandaise:** In a clean bowl, whisk 1 teaspoon of warm water or an extra egg yolk, then slowly drizzle the broken sauce in while whisking rapidly.
