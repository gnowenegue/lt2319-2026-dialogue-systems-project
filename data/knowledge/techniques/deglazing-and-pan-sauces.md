# Deglazing and Pan Sauces Techniques

### What Deglazing Means
- **Definition:** Pouring a cool or room-temperature liquid into a hot pan after browning meat or vegetables, then using a wooden spoon to scrape up and dissolve the caramelized bits stuck to the bottom.
- **What is fond:** The brown bits stuck to the pan bottom are called "fond" (French for "base"). Fond is packed with concentrated umami and savory flavors from the Maillard reaction.

### Liquids to Use for Deglazing
- **Best liquids:** Dry white wine, dry red wine, chicken or beef broth, cider, beer, or even plain water.
- **Liquids to avoid:** Avoid liquids high in dairy or sugar (like sweet cream or honey sauces) until after the pan has been deglazed and reduced, as sugars burn and dairy curdles on bare hot metal.

### How to Build a Simple Pan Sauce (Three Steps)
1. **Deglaze:** After removing cooked meat to rest on a plate, pour 1/2 cup of liquid (wine or broth) into the hot pan over medium heat. Scrape all brown bits off the pan bottom until clean.
2. **Reduce:** Let the liquid simmer and bubble rapidly until reduced by half or three-quarters (it will look syrupy).
3. **Mount with butter ("Monter au Beurre"):** Turn off the heat completely. Drop in 1–2 tablespoons of cold butter and swirl or whisk gently until melted into a glossy, velvety sauce.
