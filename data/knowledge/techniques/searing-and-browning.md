# Searing and Browning Techniques

### The Maillard Reaction and Surface Dryness
- **Why dryness matters:** The Maillard reaction (caramelization of proteins and sugars that produces deep savory flavor and crust) only occurs above 140°C (285°F). Water boils at 100°C (212°F). If meat surface is wet, surface heat goes entirely into boiling off moisture rather than browning.
- **Rule of thumb:** Always pat meat, poultry, or fish thoroughly dry with paper towels before seasoning and placing in a hot pan.

### Searing Without Sticking
- **The golden rule of searing:** Do not try to move, flip, or nudge the meat immediately after placing it in the hot pan.
- **Natural release:** As the protein crust develops and browns, it naturally releases itself from the pan surface. If it resists when you gently lift with tongs or a spatula, leave it alone for another 1–2 minutes until it releases cleanly.

### Choosing the Right Skillet for Searing
- **Best cookware:** Stainless steel, cast iron, or carbon steel skillets retain high heat and promote fond formation.
- **Avoid non-stick for searing:** Non-stick Teflon coatings break down above 260°C (500°F) and do not produce a tight golden fond for pan sauces.
