# Boiling and Simmering Techniques

### Simmer vs. Boil Differences
- **Bare Simmer (80°C–85°C / 180°F–185°F):** Tiny bubbles occasionally rise to the surface. Ideal for delicate stocks, poaching fish, and braising tender cuts without toughening proteins.
- **Gentle Simmer (85°C–95°C / 185°F–205°F):** Small, steady streams of bubbles breaking the surface across the entire pot. Standard for soups, stews, and reducing sauces.
- **Rolling Boil (100°C / 212°F):** Vigorous, violent churning bubbles that do not stop when stirred. Used for cooking dried pasta and blanching green vegetables.

### Salting Water for Pasta
- **Salinity rule:** Add roughly 1 to 1.5 tablespoons of kosher salt per 4 quarts (1 gallon) of boiling water. The water should taste distinctly seasoned, like a mild broth.
- **Why it matters:** Pasta absorbs salted water as starch granules swell. Seasoning the cooking water is the only opportunity to flavor the inside of the pasta dough.

### Why to Reserve Starchy Pasta Water
- **The secret to glossy sauces:** Always scoop out 1/2 cup of hot pasta water before draining pasta into a colander.
- **Function:** Starch suspended in the cooking water binds the cooking fat (oil or butter) with the sauce, creating a smooth, velvety emulsion that clings to the pasta instead of pooling at the bottom of the plate.
