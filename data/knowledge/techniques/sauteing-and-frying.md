# Sautéing and Pan Frying Techniques

### What Sautéing Means and Temperature Control
- **Definition:** Cooking food quickly in a small amount of oil or fat over medium-high to high heat while stirring or tossing frequently.
- **How to tell the pan is ready:** Heat the empty pan over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, then add cooking oil. The oil should "shimmer" (rippling gently across the surface). If it starts smoking, remove from heat immediately to cool down.
- **Sautéing aromatics (garlic & onions):** Sauté onions first until translucent (3–5 minutes), then add minced garlic for only 30–60 seconds until fragrant. Garlic burns quickly and becomes bitter if added too early.

### Preventing Overcrowding in the Pan
- **The problem:** When too much wet meat or vegetables are crowded into a pan, moisture cannot evaporate. The pan drops in temperature, and the food steams in its own juices instead of browning.
- **Rule of thumb:** Leave space between pieces so heat can circulate. If necessary, cook in batches and combine at the end.

### Sweating vs. Sautéing
- **Sweating:** Cooking vegetables over low heat with a pinch of salt, covered or uncovered, until softened and translucent with zero browning.
- **Sautéing:** Cooking over medium-high heat uncovered to achieve golden edges and caramelization.
